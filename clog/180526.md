# 2026-05-18

## 14:30 — CheckThat! 2025 Task 2 PoC eval (claim extraction)

Built a small eval harness for evaluating the claim extraction step against the CheckThat! 2025 Task 2 English dev split (gold-standard, human-annotated post → normalized claim pairs). Lives at `eval/scripts/checkthat_t2/`.

**Setup:** GPT-OSS 120B (Groq), 100 EN dev posts, 6 systems compared:
- `ours` — single-claim adaptation of `pipeline/claim_extraction.py`
- `dfkinit2b` — #1 EN leaderboard team's published zero-shot prompt (Figure 7 of their notebook paper)
- `dsgt` — #2 EN leaderboard team's prompt (CoT, "let's think step by step")
- `akcit` — AKCIT-FN zero-shot prompt (fidelity-focused)
- `tifin` — TIFIN 5W1H structured JSON prompt
- `baseline` — post-as-is (identity transform)

**Results (METEOR, N=100):**
- ours: 0.2934
- dsgt: 0.2764
- dfkinit2b: 0.2759
- akcit: 0.2662
- tifin: 0.2617
- baseline: 0.1870

Our prompt edged out the top published EN prompts by ~0.016-0.017 METEOR. Margin is at the threshold of meaningful at N=100 — suggestive, not proven.

**Deeper insight (more interesting than the scores):** METEOR rewards *literal phrase preservation*, not semantic accuracy. dfkinit2b's structured-criteria prompt produces near-verbatim extractions from the source post, which scores 0.80-0.98 when the gold annotation is itself a verbatim lift — but loses when the gold is a synthesized summary. Our prompt is the opposite: it abstracts/synthesizes, losing on extractive golds but winning when the gold requires picking the right specifics.

**Implication:** What "good extraction" means depends on downstream use. For Tier 3 verification we want decontextualized claims (ours-style). For embedding cache lookup (IDEA-003) verbatim might match better.

**Notable gotcha:** GPT-OSS 120B is a reasoning model on Groq — emits hidden reasoning tokens before content. Bumped max_tokens from 400 → 2000 to avoid TIFIN's JSON output truncating. Adds 0.5-1.5s latency. Worth knowing for production.

**Limitations:** N=100 (CI ~±0.02), single model, English only. We did NOT replicate the actual leaderboard winners' full systems (retrieval/ensemble/fine-tuning) — only their published zero-shot prompts. Full leaderboard top EN scores are ~0.45 from those richer systems.

Report at `eval/scripts/checkthat_t2/results/REPORT.md`. Predictions JSONL at `eval/scripts/checkthat_t2/results/predictions_100.jsonl`.

## 15:10 — Forward note for the claims DB (IDEA-003)

The eval surfaced a useful asymmetry: a verbatim/extractive claim and a synthesized/decontextualized claim describe the same fact differently, and each is better for a different downstream purpose. When we start building the claims DB, **consider storing BOTH** for each verified claim:
- The verbatim/extractive form (closer to how it appears in the wild — better for matching incoming posts that quote or paraphrase the original wording)
- The synthesized/decontextualized form (cleaner, standalone — better for matching semantically similar but differently-worded posts, and easier for downstream verification reasoning)

Both versions get embedded and indexed; link them via metadata (shared claim_id) so retrieval can hit either and resolve to the same verdict. This gives us coverage across both "paraphrase-match" and "wording-match" failure modes, and lets us later study which form actually drives more cache hits in the wild. Cheap to add at DB design time, painful to retrofit.
