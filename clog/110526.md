### Key finding: opposite blind spots

The models tie at 40% but diverge on 4 claims (1, 3, 6, 9). The divergence is directional:

- **Llama 4 Scout commits to Supported more readily** — gets claims 1 (Gimenez) and 6 (Nigeria GDP) where 3.3-70b abstains with NEE. Lower threshold for positive assertion.
- **Llama 3.3-70B commits to Refuted more readily** — gets claims 3 (NZ Food Bill) and 9 (Antifa wildfire) where 4-scout abstains with NEE. More willing to call something false from limited evidence.

4-scout: better at confirming supported facts. 3.3-70b: better at flagging refuted claims.

### Retrieval failures dominate

Claims 2, 4, 7, 8 both models get wrong — all genuine retrieval failures (1 item, 2 items, Wikipedia 429 errors). These are not model failures; both models are correctly uncertain. These same claims failed in Baseline 1.

Wikipedia 429 rate limiting hit 4 consecutive times (claims 7–10). Backoff/retry is still needed.

### Implication: simple ensemble could outperform either model alone

Take Scout's verdict when it says Supported; take 70B's verdict when it says Refuted; default to NEE when both agree. This would flip claims 1, 3, 6, 9 to correct — potentially 8/10 = 80% on this sample. Worth testing.

## 11:xx — SC 200-claim post-mortem (seed=0)

### Per-label breakdown (gold → predicted)

| Gold | n | correct | recall | dominant error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Refuted | 123 | 82 | 66.7% | 35× NEI (28% of class) |
| Supported | 55 | 34 | 61.8% | 11× NEI, 6× Refuted, 4× CE |
| NEI | 10 | 6 | 60.0% | 4× Refuted |
| Conflicting Evidence | 12 | 0 | **0.0%** | 6× Refuted, 5× NEI, 1× Supported |

Overall: 122/200 = **61%**. ECE = 0.3455 (severe overconfidence — mean post_confidence 0.95 vs accuracy 0.61).

### Comparison to FIRE baseline (30/50 = 60%, seed=42)

| Metric | FIRE (n=50, s=42) | SC (n=200, s=0) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall acc | 60% | 61% | +1pp (n.s., different seeds/n) |
| Refuted recall | 71% | 67% | −4pp |
| Supported recall | 43% | 62% | **+19pp** |
| NEI fraction of errors | 14/20 = 70% | 51/78 = 65% | −5pp |
| CE recall | — | 0% | unchanged failure |

**SC didn't move overall accuracy** but reshaped the error profile: ~half the Supported claims that FIRE incorrectly punted to NEI are now correctly Supported. Refuted recall slips slightly — averaging 3 runs makes the verifier marginally more cautious on borderline-refuted claims. Net wash on accuracy, but more *useful* errors (commits to a verdict instead of abstaining).

### NEI over-prediction: still the dominant failure mode

- 57/200 (28.5%) predictions are NEI; only 10/200 (5%) gold-NEI.
- 51/57 NEI predictions are wrong. 35 are Refuted claims, 11 Supported, 5 CE.
- The verifier defaults to NEI when it can't extract a clean signal from retrieved evidence — retrieval is doing the work, not the verdict aggregation. SC averages over Likert scores from the *same* evidence set, so it can't recover from retrieval failure.

### Conflicting Evidence: 0% recall

Of 12 CE-gold claims: 6 predicted Refuted, 5 NEI, 1 Supported, 0 CE. Predictions to CE: only 6 total across the whole sample, and all 6 were wrong-class. The verifier essentially never selects CE.
Likely cause: averaging 3 runs' ConflictingEvidence Likert scores rarely makes CE the argmax — Supported/Refuted/NEI scores dominate. The verdict-selection rule (argmax of averaged Likert) systematically suppresses CE.

### Vote-distribution analysis: not possible from current logs

`verify_claim_sc` builds `vote_str` (e.g. "Refuted×2, NEI×1") and prepends it to the reasoning string, but `eval_claims.csv` only stores `post_verdict` / `post_confidence` / `correct` — reasoning is dropped. **Action item:** if vote-distribution analysis is wanted, evaluate.py needs to persist reasoning (or at least extract and log the SC vote tuple) into eval_claims.csv. Not doing this now per session constraint (no SC-logic changes).

### Implications

1. **NEI is the bottleneck, not verifier disagreement.** SC at n=3 doesn't help where retrieval gives the verifier nothing to commit on. Improving retrieval (BM25 hybrid, IDEA-008) likely dominates further SC tuning.
2. **The argmax-of-averaged-Likert rule kills CE.** Worth testing majority-vote-of-verdicts as an alternative aggregation (cheap to add, doesn't change verify_claim itself).
3. **Calibration is broken** — confidence ~0.95 with 0.61 accuracy, ECE 0.35. Temperature scaling on the 200-claim labeled set is now viable; this is a higher-leverage next step than another SC variant.
4. **Same-seed FIRE-vs-SC** still needed for a clean Δ. Current comparison straddles two seeds and two sample sizes.
